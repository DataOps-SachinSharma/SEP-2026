For the last few years, enterprises have been experimenting with AI.We built chatbots. We connected documents to LLMs. We created copilots. We tested different models and asked AI to summarise, generate, analyse, and explain.
But something important is changing. AI is moving from answering questions to performing work. The next generation of enterprise AI won't simply tell an employee what to do. It will understand context, access authorised business data, call the right tools, execute multi-step workflows, and return an outcome. This is where platforms such as Microsoft Foundry become important.
Consider a simple finance scenario. A user might ask, “Why did our expenses increase this month?” A traditional AI assistant may provide a general explanation based on its existing knowledge. An enterprise AI system connected to authorised business data can take a very different approach. It can retrieve the organisation's financial information, compare the current period with previous periods, identify accounts with significant changes, analyse transaction patterns, and explain the major drivers of the increase. The result is no longer just generated information. It is an analysis based on the organisation's actual business context. For organisations using Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, this evolution creates particularly interesting possibilities. AI can potentially work with financial data, customer information, vendor transactions, inventory, invoices, purchasing, reporting, and other business processes. A finance professional could ask a natural-language question and receive an explanation based on real business data rather than a generic AI response. Over time, these capabilities can extend from insight generation toward workflow assistance, exception handling, analysis, and controlled execution.
